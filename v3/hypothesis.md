Experiment: Synthetic Delay–Driven Data Continuity During Camera Outages
Objective

Evaluate whether a receiver-side fallback mechanism that uses historical image data and synthetic interarrival delays can maintain continuous federated learning during camera outages while preserving realistic resource utilization.

The experiment aims to demonstrate that:

Training can continue despite temporary loss of live camera feeds.
Synthetic delay models (KDE and WGAN) can reproduce realistic data arrival behavior during outages.
CPU utilization remains closer to normal operating conditions than when historical data is replayed at a fixed or uncontrolled rate.
Model accuracy degradation is minimized compared to stopping training entirely.
Background

Previous work established the following pipeline:

UA-DETRAC images were streamed from a sender VM to a receiver VM using UDP.
The receiver recorded packet/image interarrival times.
Interarrival traces were used to train:
Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) model
Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) model
Synthetic delays generated by these models were incorporated into a federated-learning simulation to model realistic data arrival behavior.

This experiment extends that work by introducing camera outages.

System Architecture
Sender Nodes

Three sender nodes represent traffic cameras. Note that for the sake of simplicity in this research work, we'll have one
sender VM running three sender processes.

Each sender:

Streams UA-DETRAC images to the receiver.
Uses its assigned image sequence.
Transmits using UDP.
Produces live image traffic under normal operation.
Receiver

The receiver:

Collects incoming images.
Performs dataset buffering.
Trains the learning model.
Detects camera outages.
Switches to historical image replay when live data is unavailable.

The receiver is therefore responsible for maintaining training continuity.

Proposed Condition
Camera Outage

At predefined times:

One or more sender nodes stop transmitting.
Receiver detects missing data.
Receiver activates fallback mode.
Fallback Mode

Instead of waiting indefinitely for missing images:

Receiver retrieves images from a historical repository.
Images are injected into the training pipeline.
Injection timing is controlled by synthetic delays.

The receiver therefore behaves as if data is still arriving from the failed camera.

Fallback Strategies
Strategy A: No Fallback

When a camera fails:

Data stream stops.
Training receives fewer samples.
Missing data is not replaced.

Purpose:

Reference condition.
Strategy B: Historical Replay Without Delay Modeling

When a camera fails:

Historical images are replayed immediately or at a fixed rate.

Expected behavior:

Sudden burst of arrivals.
Increased CPU utilization.
Potential queue growth.
Unrealistic network behavior.
Strategy C: Historical Replay Using KDE Delays

When a camera fails:

Historical images are selected.
Delay between consecutive images is sampled from KDE-generated delays.
Images are replayed according to those delays.

Expected behavior:

Arrival process resembles real traffic.
Resource utilization remains stable.
Strategy D: Historical Replay Using WGAN Delays

When a camera fails:

Historical images are selected.
Delay between consecutive images is sampled from WGAN-generated delays.
Images are replayed according to those delays.

Expected behavior:

Better preservation of burstiness and temporal structure.
Most realistic replacement traffic.

Hypotheses
H1

Camera outages reduce data availability and negatively impact training when no fallback mechanism exists.

H2

Historical replay without delay modeling creates artificial bursts that increase CPU utilization and queue buildup.

H3

Synthetic-delay replay produces resource consumption patterns closer to normal operation.

H4

WGAN-generated delays better preserve burstiness and therefore more closely reproduce real system behavior than KDE-generated delays.

H5

Maintaining data continuity through synthetic-delay replay preserves training effectiveness better than allowing training to stall or operate with reduced data.

Expected Contribution

The experiment demonstrates that synthetic communication traces are useful not only for network simulation but also for maintaining operational continuity in distributed learning systems.

Specifically, synthetic delays can be used to:

Sustain training during temporary camera outages.
Preserve realistic arrival behavior.
Prevent resource spikes caused by naive historical replay.
Maintain learning performance while data sources are unavailable.
